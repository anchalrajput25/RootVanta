# Step 8 results: does it work, how fast, how cheap, how many users

Measured on 15 October 2026. Every number here can be reproduced with the
command next to it.

## 1. Finding the right cause (practice incidents)

`python -m incident_agent.evals.practice --cases 1000 --seed 2026`

1,000 generated incidents in small made-up systems (4 to 7 services). Each
one has a known cause: a bad deploy of the alerting service, a bad deploy of
a service it calls, a deploy that only made it slow, a failing dependency,
overload, or nothing wrong. Distractions are mixed in: a harmless deploy
elsewhere, old noisy errors, a one-off spike, a slow start, missing traces or
logs, and log text that tries to give orders.

The first run showed real weaknesses, which were then fixed (see below).
The fixes were made using seed 7. The numbers below are from seed 2026, which
was not used while fixing.

| | Before step 8 | After step 8 |
|---|---|---|
| Named the right cause | 53.1% | **88.9%** |
| Confidently wrong (named a wrong cause) | 16.6% | **1.8%** |
| Said "not enough evidence" | 30.3% | 9.3% |
| Right cause on top (AC@1) | 66.0% | 89.8% |
| Right cause in top 3 (AC@3) | 87.4% | 91.2% |
| False alarms on the 148 healthy cases | 0 | 0 |
| Right fix, when the cause was right | 100% | 100% |
| Time per investigation (p50 / p95) | 39 / 62 ms | 40 / 60 ms |

By kind of problem (after):

| Problem | Right | Unsure | Wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bad deploy of the alerting service | 91% | 9% | 0% |
| Bad deploy of a service it calls | 72% | 20% | 8% |
| Deploy that only made it slow | 92% | 8% | 0% |
| Failing dependency | 85% | 13% | 2% |
| Overload | 97% | 3% | 0% |
| Nothing wrong | 100% | 0% | 0% |

What the benchmark found and what changed:

1. **A deploy to a service further down the chain was blamed on "that service is
   down"** (80% wrong). The fix it led to was "route around it" instead of
   "roll it back". Now a failing dependency that was itself just deployed is
   treated as a bad deploy of that dependency (`rules.py`).
2. **A deploy that made a fast service slow was missed.** A rise from 60 ms to 200 ms
   stayed under the fixed 300 ms line. Latency now also counts as abnormal at
   2x its normal level and at least 100 ms more. A deploy followed within
   3 minutes by problems in the same service now counts as evidence, even
   with no new error messages (`collectors.py`, `rules.py`).
3. **One noisy sample started an incident.** A value must now stay abnormal for
   two samples (30 s) in a row.

Still weak, honestly: with **no logs** the agent cannot see deploys at all
(80 cases: 38% right, 19% wrong), and without traces it often stays unsure
about dependencies. These cases are generated by this project, so they are a
practice test, not independent proof. RCAEval (section 4) is the independent
check.

## 2. Speed and AI cost

- The rule engine needs about **40 ms** per investigation (p95 60 ms), not
  counting the time to read from monitoring.
- From alert to finished investigation, through the queue and real HTTP calls
  to monitoring: **1.3 to 1.9 s** (p50) under normal load (section 3).
- The optional AI step sends about **2,000 tokens** per investigation (p95
  2,700, estimated at 4 characters per token, as the AI was not called here).
  With 1,000 to 4,000 tokens back, one investigation costs about:
  - Claude Opus 5.5: $0.03 to $0.09
  - Claude Sonnet 5.5: $0.014 to $0.045
  - Claude Haiku 5.5: under $0.003
- **New:** every investigation now records its time, and when the AI step runs,
  its real token counts and cost (`investigation_metrics` table). The dashboard
  shows typical investigation time and this month's AI cost; each report shows
  its own. Prices are in `investigation/llm.py`.

## 3. Many companies and people at once (load test)

`python -m incident_agent.loadtest --api http://localhost:8000 --companies 50 --people 10 --seconds 60 --think 4`

Each person keeps using the dashboard (one click every 2 to 6 s, about what
the dashboard's own refresh does), while each company's monitoring sends an
alert about every 15 s. Investigations run for real against a built-in fake
Prometheus/Loki/Jaeger. Setup: PostgreSQL with row-level security, 4 API
processes, 4 workers, all on **one 4-core machine that also ran the load
generator and the database**.

| People at once | Companies | Requests/s | List incidents p50 / p95 | Alert to finished investigation p50 / p95 | Server errors | Data seen by another company |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 10 | 19 | 13 / 55 ms | 1.3 / 2.8 s | 0 | 0 |
| 250 | 25 | 50 | 49 / 59 ms | 1.4 / 2.7 s | 0 | 0 |
| 500 | 50 | 122 | 56 / 1,760 ms | 1.9 / 6.6 s | 2 (dropped connections) | 0 |
| 1,000 | 100 | 125 | 2,828 / 15,317 ms | 15.5 / 36.0 s | 0 | 0 |

What this means:

- **This one small machine serves about 250 active people with fast pages.** It
  tops out at about **125 requests per second**. At 500 people the slowest
  pages slow down, and at 1,000 everything queues. Nothing broke: every
  investigation still finished.
- **Company data never leaked**, in 625 checks across all runs, even when
  overloaded.
- The API keeps no state between requests, so the limit grows by adding API
  processes or machines. The database was not the bottleneck: the API
  processes were, at about 100% CPU each.
- One change from this test: checking who is signed in, done on every request,
  now takes one database query instead of two (`api/deps.py`).

## 4. RCAEval, the public benchmark

`python -m incident_agent.evals.rcaeval --download re1 --data data/rcaeval`
(or double-click `run_benchmarks.bat`)

RCAEval (WWW 2025) has 735 real failure cases from three demo systems. A fault
is injected into one service, and the task is to rank that service first.
Two methods are scored the same way:

- **BARO**, a published method (FSE 2024), rebuilt here from RCAEval's code. On
  RCAEval's own sample case it gives the same top answers as their notebook.
- **ours**: the same idea, plus the investigator's rules: a change must last
  3 samples, and error logs and the place where failing traces start are
  combined in.

Run on 15 October 2026 on the user's PC, all 735 cases (RE1 has metrics only;
RE2 and RE3 also have logs and traces):

| Suite | Cases | BARO AC@1 | BARO Avg@5 | ours AC@1 | ours Avg@5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RE1-OB (Online Boutique) | 125 | 0.72 | 0.87 | 0.72 | 0.87 |
| RE1-SS (Sock Shop) | 125 | 0.86 | 0.96 | 0.86 | 0.96 |
| RE1-TT (Train Ticket) | 125 | 0.56 | 0.81 | 0.57 | 0.81 |
| RE2-OB | 90 | 0.32 | 0.80 | **0.23** | **0.76** |
| RE2-SS | 90 | 0.14 | 0.77 | 0.14 | 0.77 |
| RE2-TT | 90 | 0.44 | 0.78 | 0.44 | 0.78 |
| RE3-OB | 30 | 0.03 | 0.71 | **0.00** | **0.49** |
| RE3-SS | 30 | 0.07 | 0.68 | 0.07 | 0.67 |
| RE3-TT | 30 | 0.47 | 0.88 | 0.47 | 0.88 |
| All | 735 | 0.50 | 0.83 | 0.49 | 0.82 |

What this means, honestly:

- On metrics alone (RE1) we are level with BARO: the faulty service is first
  in about 7 of 10 cases and in the top 5 almost 9 of 10 times.
- Adding logs and traces did **not** help. On Online Boutique it made things
  worse (RE2-OB 0.32 to 0.23, RE3-OB Avg@5 0.71 to 0.49). On Sock Shop and
  Train Ticket it changed almost nothing, which suggests their log and trace
  service names mostly did not match the metric names, so the extra rankings
  were not used.
- RE2 and RE3 are much harder for both methods (0.03 to 0.47 first-place),
  because many faults there (code bugs, disk, socket) barely move CPU or
  latency.
- Overall: 0.49 vs 0.50 first-place, 0.82 vs 0.83 top-5. Level with a recent
  published method, not better.

Next fix (not done yet, so these numbers stay the real ones): weight logs and
traces only when they point to one service clearly, check the name matching
for Sock Shop and Train Ticket, and test the change on part of the cases
before scoring it on the rest, so the result is not tuned to the test.

## 5. What milestone 10 changed

Alert grouping, the dependency map and incident memory change what people see,
not how causes are ranked, so the benchmark numbers above still stand. The one
ranking change is that a value is now also abnormal when it is far outside that
service's own usual spread (8 robust standard deviations), not only above the
fixed limits. On the practice benchmark that moved overload from 97% to 100%
right and left everything else unchanged (1,000 cases, seed 2026: 89.3% right,
1.8% confidently wrong, 0 false alarms on the 148 healthy cases).

## 6. Safety (unchanged)

`python -m incident_agent.evals.runner`: the false-success rate is 0%
(baseline 100%), and 0 unsafe actions were executed. 234 automatic tests pass
on PostgreSQL.
